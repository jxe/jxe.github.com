# First selection notes

## The book found in this batch

The archive reads less like a collection of conventional lineated poems than a sequence of prose poems, comic lectures, lists, and travel dispatches. Its most distinctive mode is ecstatic attention: the speaker invents rituals for entering a place, stares too hard at food and weather, and lets technology, hospitality, appetite, and metaphysics leak into one another.

That suggests a provisional title, **The Crab Is All**, and a compact movement:

1. **Thresholds** — looking at the world, then learning how to enter it
2. **Appetites** — attention expressed through food and offering
3. **Altitude** — friendship and cosmology seen from the mountains
4. **Return** — copies, reality, and one last total act of appetite

The sequence is thematic rather than strictly chronological.

## In the first manuscript

### The Airplane

The best opening in the archive. Its spy fiction gives distance from the autobiographical speaker, while the dill and day-night map establish the book's obsessive attention and its fusion of travel with technology.

### Sit Down—Again

A strong statement of method disguised as practical advice. The image of pews everywhere turns passivity into social technique and gives the book a humane way of crossing thresholds.

### H2O—the Original Ninja Molecule

Compact, funny, and formally alive. The status-report structure lets romance, weather, biochemistry, Linux, and longing occupy one plane without explanation.

### The Meal

One of the archive's clearest acts of ecstatic attention. The technical list accumulates into feeling, and the surrounding hill-station setting deepens rather than merely explains the food.

### The Mango

Extracted from “Tidbits.” It is a complete prose poem by itself: mock scripture, physics, botany, and the final physical joke all serve one escalating image.

### An Omelet

Pairs culinary precision with a table of vividly different people. The questionnaire following it in the email concerns an earlier piece and is omitted.

### We're All on Mountains

Retitled from “My Friends Are So Fat, I Mean Cool.” Its mountaintop coincidence and catalog of friends supply a generous, outward-looking emotional center.

### The Sensory Perception of God

A small ars poetica for the whole collection: God is perceived in warmth, silence, harmonics, and motion, then placed improbably beside science and Busta Rhymes.

### The Labyrinth of Imitation

The archive's strongest conceptual essay-poem. Its recurrences and doublings make tourism, capital, narration, and authenticity part of the same trap. It needs factual and cultural review before publication.

### The Crab

The natural closer and source of the provisional title. It turns appetite into metaphysical wager, makes social embarrassment heroic, and ends the trip with a clean unresolved question.

## Strong alternates

- **Journal Fragments** — several excellent miniatures, especially the roaring child and the stamping officials; better mined as individual fragments than included whole.
- **Sit Down** — contains the original “aggressive patience” insight, but becomes didactic where “Sit Down—Again” enacts the idea.
- **Praise the Lord** — the Sufi/bandwidth premise is memorable, but the satire and generalizations need careful rereading.
- **Paskal** — warm and substantial, with the beautiful idea of carrying a home through daily hospitality; possibly valuable if the larger corpus supports that theme.
- **Keren** — real lyric urgency and good self-questioning, but repetition and private portraiture currently outweigh its best sentences.
- **Tibetan Buddhism or STAR TREK** — a sustained and very readable conceit; held back because its joke risks flattening a living religious tradition into exotic scenery.

## Held back for now

- **Hong Kong** — extends the charming spy conceit but is looser and less surprising than “The Airplane.”
- **Useful Hindi Phrases** — ends well, but its setup contains cultural framing that has aged poorly.
- **Elite Indian Foods for Muscular Men** — energetic, but the gender shtick feels more dated than necessary.
- **Snakes, Coals, and Soldering Irons** — vivid but structurally diffuse, with portrayals and language that would require major ethical revision.
- **Performance Chow-Mein Chefs of Uttar Pradesh** — a strong opening scene gives way to broad claims about India.
- **Sunita** — intimate and overdetermined; it exposes another person without yet becoming a fully independent poem.
- **More News** — two unrelated dispatches, one of them product feedback rather than literary material.
- **Lisa** — has an interesting theory of receptivity, but the portrait's claims about its subject feel both private and diminishing.
- **Leave No Charred Remains** — the opening variations have comic force, but the cremation-ground material is glib in a way the present manuscript cannot carry.

## Questions for the expanded corpus

- Is this really an India book, or will the later poems reveal a larger lifelong book about attention and appetite?
- Does “The Crab Is All” remain the right title once the Dropbox work arrives?
- Should the final book retain prose blocks, or use selective lineation only where rhythm demands it?
- Which real names should remain, be changed, or be used only with permission?
- How much of the period's abrasive comic voice belongs in the finished work?
