
Tibetan Buddhism or STAR TREK:  You decide!

They wear robes & togas in solid colors like maroon, purple, and orange, and
they meander clockwise around long, pillared open-air buildings which jut
out from the sides of impossibly steep mountains covered with lush green
vegetation.

They carry musical instruments made of unrecognizable shiny metals, with
strange runic inscriptions written on them.  They use these musical
instruments to purify spaces, playing into them before ceremonies, and
several times a day they play them over the railings of their long,
pillared, open-air buildings, echoing in the valleys of the impossibly steep
mountains, below.

Walking in the wilderness in their land, you are surprised to find brass
cylinders, with the same runic characters written on them, marvelously
engineered to turn endlessly and at a steady rate in even the slightest
breeze.  You find these devices, some much taller than a man, some the size
of a drinking glass, always rotating clockwise, on the peak of every
mountain and at regular intervals on every path.

Their music is full of microtones and harmonics.  They sing so low that it
feels like the earth shaking.  During sunset, the sound of hundreds of
people clapping sporadically is heard, and when you go to investigate, you
find that the young men in their maroon robes are drilling each other and
having logical debates, clapping their hands loudly every time they offer a
point to find fault in or an unanswerable question that traps the other
person.

The things they value most highly are the practice of logic, and that the
good of all beings shall take precedence over the good of the one.

In their cafeteria I ordered Earl Grey, hot, and a Roquefort & walnut pizza.
Not only did they know what I was talking about, I received both almost
immediately.

They teach that there are many planets and levels of reality, and that in a
future evolution of humanity we will be able to move through them at will,
and operate in many places simultaneously, and to 'manifest' as whatever we
like-- taking different shapes as the need arises.  Our 'mindstreams' will
be able to commingle, and we will be able to know each other's thoughts and
dreams, and we will be able to work for the good of all beings, answering
any and all distress calls that come our way.

Tibetan Buddhism or STAR TREK:  You decide!

        Joe
