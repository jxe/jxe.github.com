I've spent the last week living with a lovely traditional family in a
suburb of Jaipur in Rajasthan.  They've taught me a lot of Hindi and I've
taught them a lot of English.  We spend the afternoons riding over sand
dunes in motorbikes.  I can drive a motorcycle now.  And the morning we
spend in the shade eating delicious food prepared for us by various veiled
women, none of whom ever speak, or playing cricket.  It's a good life.

One thing that surprised me in Nepal and here in India is the number of
spot-welding tools.  Every family has at least one of them.  Everyone
makes (and remakes) their own metal tools, here in the third world.

I have also learned to eat with my hands in a sanitary way.

Today my family came into the city and got me on a bus to Pushkar, where
I will try to find some fire dancers to befriend.  I am enjoying myself.

Things I can say in Hindi:

	"I would like to help your wife prepare the meal."  "Come here and
	sit with me."  "I am not a tourist.  I am a musician."	"You claim
	to be my friend but we have just met.  You are a fraud."
