In Scotland (and for that matter, in Boston and San Francisco), I
found that sitting down, in churches, lead to my rapid induction into
a supportive community.  Just by sitting in a pew for a time, I was
noticed, and people would approach me and introduce themselves, smile,
and try to help me.

I've always liked the idea of church travel-- of visiting a far away
congregation.  Churches are a very highly evolved kind of distributed
community:  a sort of global network of largely independent cells that
are none-the-less capable of surprising coordination and mobility,
like a network of secret societies or an underground railroad that is
actually above-ground and well-known.

But India had me stumped, for a while.  There are very few churches,
and, for mostly-obvious reasons, Hindi temples don't suffice.  I had been
preparing to try my luck with Sufis or Sikhs when I had a breakthrough.

There are pews *everywhere*.

For each of the last three days, between the hours of 5 and 6 in the
evening, I have sat on a rock wall facing a street near both an elementary
school and a university.  Families pass me after picking up their children
at the elementary school.  University students pass me on their way out to
dinner.  I smile, occasionally say "namaste", make faces at the children,
act tough with the college boys, and act shy about the college girls.
On the first day, they smiled back.  On the second day, they began to sit
with me, ask me my name, compliment my new haircut, and turn around as
they walked away and wave.  Today, they began to invite me to come along.
