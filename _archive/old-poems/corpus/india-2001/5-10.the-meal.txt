The meal began with the observation, casual at first, that there were
three varieties of rice on my plate.

It ended with observation that there were thin sugar crystals, artfully
and abstractly shaped, scattered through the roasted anise seeds that
were brought me as breath-freshener, and that this scattering of sugar
crystals was fantastic.  That I had never experienced anything like it.

Of the rice, I will only say that two of the varieties would be called,
in the west, basmati.  One of them had shorter grains than the other.
They were entirely different.  Worlds apart, really.  The shorter kind was
served fried quickly in butter strewn with tiny toasted anise seeds and
bits of wilted lettuce or celery leaf.  The third kind was in the rice
pudding, which I cannot describe.  The grains of this rice were thin,
up to an inch long, and very yellow.

I am only prepared at this point to list general descriptions of each
course.  The emotions are still too powerful for me to go into it in
more depth.

CLOCKWISE in small metal bowls:

Dish #1:  (veg) cumin, cilantro, salt on stir-fried cauliflower & red
potato with strips of tomato skin and spinach.

Dish #2:  cheese (like a swiss/mozz cross) & peas in a parsley & bay leaf
gravy, thickened with lentil flour, flavored w. burnt apples, onions,
& mushrooms.

Dish #3:  (dal) orange lentils crushed in an oil in which tomatoes and
red peppers have been slowly cooked, thickened with saffron and poured
on parsley.

Dish #4:  whole chickpeas & shallots in a dark fiery pepper, thyme,
coriander & wine gravy w. MSG.

Dish #5:  (salad) grilled squash & tomatillos pickled in lemon juice &
red chillis, with some of those little black spheres you find in dill
pickle jars.


I am in Nainital, summer resort of the Indian upper class.  I am the only
white person here, but most of the signs are in English, because the upper
class takes a perverse pride in the fact that even middle class citizens
can't navigate this town.  There is a yacht club here and a few boats,
and cricket games, and one can rent pedal boats and pedal around the
large lake.  Many people ride horses through the city streets and on the
trails around it.  I am at an elevation of 7,400 feet.  The hotels here
are imitations of Scottish castles and enormous Swiss cottages.  The air
is crisp and cool.  The internet access is managed by mysterious sufis.
The women are educated, many of them in foreign countries, and are
willing to speak to me.

And this town caters to Indians, not to European tourists, and that
means that the prices are very moderate, everything is very well made,
hotels are surrounded by quiet woodland, and the food... my god the
food... the food is amazing.
