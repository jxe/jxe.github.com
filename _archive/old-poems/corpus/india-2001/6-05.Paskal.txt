Praise to All of Creation & to the Hidden Men & Women who Conspire on
my Behalf! I am so HAPPY!

I am happy because I am waking every morning to the sounds of birds and
quiet, because I am often preparing food for other people, because there
are many people around me whom I admire, because I know that I am doing
good things, because I have been unusually and regularly productive, and
because things are getting really adventurous and playful around here.
This is all due to the influences of several people on my life.  I will
describe these people and their influences each in a separate email,
over the course of this week.

This note is about Paskal, a french canadian I met and spent time with
in Rishikesh, before I came to Dharamshala and was filled with joy.


PASKAL

Paskal is a master storyteller, and a master of many other things.  He has
a way of telling a story which makes him seem small, and the story large,
and happening all around him.  His eyes go wide with wonder or admiration,
he sputters and says "wow" and "phew" all the time, and he seems to see
the things he is describing before his eyes again.  It's great.  In all
things he achieves this smallness-- this constant relation to the world
around him which is vast-- and it has the marvellous effect of making
him very easy to listen to and learn from.  He is often the center of
attention or the leader, but it doesn't seem so to those around him,
because he makes himself small.

This I hope to learn from him eventually.  But already I have learned
something vital from him.  Many things, actually, because the simple thing
I learned from Paskal cascaded until it changed every aspect of my life.

You see, my acquaintance with Paskal began with him offering me
breakfast.  Immediately I thought well of him, because this act of
hospitality above everything has impressed and eluded me in my travels.
There are some travellers who manage, far from any hearth or kitchen,
to create a pleasant space in their small room or corridor, and to act
as hostess or host, and to be generous.  I had progressed to the point
where I realized that this capacity involved ownership of certain things:
cookies, candles, bottled water or juices, but I would buy these things
and then use them up, I would clean my room and arrange it for company
but then mess it up again, so that whenever the time for hospitality
seemed near, I was unprepared.  Alas!

You see, Paskal rises early every day, does some yoga, and then goes to
the market to buy fruits.  Every day!  Mangoes and bananas and lychees!
He adds them to porridge for himself and offers them to others that live
near him.  With him the possession of these articles of generosity was
not mysterious as it was with the others-- it arose naturally out of
his daily routine.  This was something I could imitate, and indeed I
began daily to rise, sing a little, go to market, clean my room, and
then offer people food.

This, my friends, was a watershed event.  It changed everything, for when
I took on this daily routine I found myself in a different space entirely.
My life is ordered and good.  It is like I have a home which I carry
with me.  And it only costs me about $2 a day to make brunch for five
or six people here.  And they reciprocate!  People give me massages,
bring me things, take me places that they think I will like, show me
their art, etc.

Paskal is back in Canada now, but every day I have reason to thank
him again.



MY SCHEDULE (for those interested)

I rise at 6:30 or 7.  By 8, I am back in my place with the fruits,
vegetables, breads, cheeses & herbs I will need for the day.  By 8am!
By 9am, I am showered and my room is cleaned and the food is prepared.
By 10am I have had breakfast with others, made new friends, perhaps tried
something new.  Next follow two 4 and a half hour time blocks, call them
A (10-2:30) & B (2:30-7). Generally in one of them I do something inside
and in the other something outside.  Generally when inside I either write
or code.  I try to get some coding done every day.  At 7pm I watch the
sun set over the himalaya.  Evenings are for collaborative music, art,
or dance, for another meal which I prepare, or for teaching Tibettan
refugees English, depending on what day of the week it is.
