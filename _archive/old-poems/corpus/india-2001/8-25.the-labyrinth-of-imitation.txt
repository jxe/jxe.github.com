8/25:  THE LABYRINTH OF IMITATION

Guidebooks of the kind travellers carry, with listings of hotels and
restaurants and so forth-- these books can have a frightening power in
India: like mirrors, they can turn the ordinary world into a labyrinth.

When the Beatles went to India, they stayed in Rishikesh.  In the area of
Rishikesh called Swargashram, one can find two restaurants side-by-side.
Both are named "Chotiwallah".  They have identical signs, identical floor
plans, and the men who greet customers out front are dressed identically.
Both restaurants claim to have been established in 1942.  Standing on the
street, it is as though one's eyes are crossed.  It seems an illusion, or a
trap.

No one remembers, anymore, which is the more authentic.

On one corner in Varanasi, you can find seven identical stores selling
yogurt drinks.  A year ago there were two.  Apparently the one on the left
was the authentic one, when there were two.  Now there are seven, and no
one remembers, anymore, which was the first.

Capital is the global solvent which dissolves all things.

In the Vishnu Rest House, there is a girl copying directly from her
guidebook into her journal.  She does not ever leave the hotel.  She does
not make anything up.  She copies each experience exactly as written.  She
orders a yoghurt drink.

But this girl, call her Anne, is in the real Varanasi, at least.  The real
Varanasi?  Well, far to the south of the city center is a second city, a
sham city.  Sometimes, when tourists arrive on the train and ask to be
taken to some place listed in their guidebook: to the Vishnu Rest House, or
the Madras Cafe, or the city center-- sometimes they are taken to this sham
city.  Like in a wax museum, everything here is close together and unreal
and silent.  Manikarnika, the place where Hindis burn their dead, is
duplicated there, but there are never any burnings today.  Goadalia, the
city center which in real life is noisy and bustling is here quiet, and
smaller.

And there are tourists living here.  They think they are in Varanasi.  They
think they're staying in the Vishnu Rest House.  They think they're eating
at the Madras Cafe.  But the real Madras Cafe is three miles to the north,
and they are living in a cheap imitation.

And there is a girl, call her Anne, who is copying directly from her
guidebook into her journal.

Are you lost in the labyrinth of imitation?
