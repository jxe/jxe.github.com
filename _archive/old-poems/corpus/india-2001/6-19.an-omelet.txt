Today I made an omelet.  It was stuffed with finely diced parsnip and
green bell pepper.  The parsnip was cooked in two stages:  first, slowly
over low heat, then with the green pepper added over very high heat.
The omelet was served on the leaves of a local plant called bol, whose
leaves are thin like those of romaine lettuce but peppery like some
kinds of chard.  The stems of these leaves I sliced and fried with
some minced garlic, and this I incorporated into the omelet batter.
I folded the omelet over the stuffing and cut it up, placed each piece
atop its bed of bol leaves, poured on some local cream, and sprinkled
with finely ground black pepper.  I put a little pile of diced tomatoes
in sugared lime juice at the side of each dish to offset the yellow-green
color scheme and to provide an additional garnish option.

I served it to a large Mongol named Dugar with only one functional leg
who escaped from China with a group of Tibetan and Tuvan monks, who
is brilliant, and who now does little but read books in every academic
subject, paint watercolors, and attempt Tuvan throat singing with me.

I served it to a petit South African installation artist and "ecological
organizer" named Karen who was in the contact improv dance workshop I
lead yesterday and who was in many ways awakened by it and who could not
stop dancing and singing and smiling and being affectionate and wild and
otherwise different than her usually conservative self after the dance.

I served it to a mid-sized American netadmin named James who will help
me tomorrow as I try to teach Buddhist monks how to program in Perl.
