3/12/01:  Today I roared like a lion at a child who was begging me for
some chocolate.  He roared right back.

3/15/01:  As I followed my sister up the mountain today, a pretty 16-year
old girl carrying a heavy load on her back approached me and suggested
we trade-- she would carry my backpack and I would carry what she had.
She was a porter in the employ of a hotel about 1,500 feet higher.
She didn't speak English very well and had that obnoxious habit of
starting every sentence with "Hello".  I agreed.  It turned out she was
carrying at least 80 pounds; and, lacking the funds for a backpack, she
used a basket held on her back by a padded rope slung over her forehead.
After she showed me where on my forehead would best support the weight, I
did good, although I found it necessary to stop several times and stretch
my neck.  It is impossible, using this device, to look left or right as
your climb the trail.  One must always looks straight ahead and down.

3/15/01:  Several times as we climbed together we were given tea
in someone's house for free, or in exchange for a cabbage which the
girl would take from the basket on my back.  People seemed very nice
although I couldn't understand a word they said.  At the girl's hotel,
her mother, who also worked there, suggested that we marry.  But I think
she was joking.

3/17/01:  Discussed teaching English in the small Himalayan village
of Shyabru.  Within an hour of my offhandedly mentioning my interest
to a resident, at least ten town and school officials had gathered
in a room with me.  Mostly they talked and argued among themselves
in Nepali.  Also they seemed to draw up and stamp, with a variety of
stamps, and very noisily, at least a dozen documents.  They wanted
me to start immediately.  They were worried about liability, should
anything happen to me.  They were prepared to offer me room and board.
They seemed tremendously authoritative.  One of them showed me to an
empty room cluttered with trash and pointed to a rusty stove, while
another followed and continued stamping documents and, I think, a few
blank scraps of paper, violently against a wall.

3/20/01: I have taken a room overlooking the old palace square in
Kathmandu.  It is small and when it rains there are roaches, but the
vantage point is terrific and I have a patio and roof access.  From my
patio I can observe a monkey that lives in the square.  This is not a
tourist place.

3/22/01: I fought with the monkey again today.  They say his name is
Baboo.  He lives in the red pagoda in Kathmandu's palace square.  He is a
red monkey.  He took my bananas.  I have been living off bananas and brown
rice, so this is a problem.  The locals laughed at me as I chased him up
and down the pagoda.  Several times he allowed me to catch up, turned,
and made faces at me.  Some were very intimidating, very threatening.
Sometimes he would hold the banana-bunch in his mouth and, with his
left hand, he would make a shooing gesture.  It reminded me of something
Robert DeNiro might do in some gangster movie.  He was through with me.
Other times he would hold the bananas in his left hand and bar his teeth,
or breathe out through pursed lips.  This reminded me more of Christopher
Walken.   He could move around that Pagoda very easily on all fours.
I was huffing and puffing.  I think this monkey could have a future in
action movies.
