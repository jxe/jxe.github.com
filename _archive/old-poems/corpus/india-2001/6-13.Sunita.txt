I met Sunita within two hours of my arrival in Dharamsala.  She was
sitting with her friend Anita at a chai shop.  In her posture I saw
something of a dancer, something of a mafia hitman.  The way she moved
fluidly between German, English, and Hindi-- each with ease and elegance--
turned me on.  Within 10 minutes it came out that she was interested in
learning contact dance and she also wanted to sing with me.  This place
is going to be alright, I thought.

Sunita reminds me of a female James Bond.  She has a very extroverted
side.  She holds herself brilliantly like a hostess at a party, and she
spins and dances all the time.  She has an easy grace and laughter with
her shoulders thrown back.  She would look at home holding a martini
glass or calling a waiter, telling a story or smiling at one, or making
eyes at a man across the room.  She has a fluid kind of motion that can
make her the center of attention and yet seem unobtrusive.

And yet she can also be surprisingly stealthy, spiritual, and
matter-of-fact.  She is an adept of tantric yoga.  She has lived in Delhi
wearing Indian clothing and blending in, infiltrating.  At age 22, she
has supported herself financially for three years.  And more and more
she is dedicating herself to things: taking everything that crosses her
path both seriously and with humor.  She is listening to people.  She is
acting as a mirror:  with people who talk, she talks.  With people who
are silent, she is silent.

It is exciting just to be near Sunita because she is on her way to so
many things.  She is on her way to nature, on her way to improvisation,
on her way to subtle art.  I can see things being born within her as
I watch.  I can see her eventual completion, shining like a golden light
beneath her skin.

It is exciting to be with Sunita because she is willing to play with
me-- to dance and make up silly songs with me, to talk with me or have
a picnic by a river with me.  She seems ready to respond to anything,
and she is thoughtful about it all.

It is exciting to know I will see her again soon, because I have so much
to learn from her.  She knows more than me about eye contact, about
touch, and about many good things having to do with people.  When it
comes to being lovely and sensitive and powerful, she is a professional,
and she is willing to teach.

I met Sunita within two hours of my arrival in Dharamsala.  I have
not recovered.  Every time I think of her, I am amazed.
