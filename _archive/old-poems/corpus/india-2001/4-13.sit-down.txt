One thing I have discovered is that aggressive patience is my most
valuable tool while travelling.  The parasites and crooks which
feed off tourists are intimidated by an obvious willingness to wait.
If you go from hotel to hotel in the middle of the night in Delhi,
folks will overcharge you something fierce.  If you sit on a curb and,
when anyone approaches you, act as if you like the curb a lot and
even invite them to join you on it, you immediately escape all the
subterfuge and manipulation.  People will invite you to stay in their
homes for free, or in their hotels at cost.  It is this simple:  when
you arrive at an airport or train station, whatever the time, sit down.
This takes some discipline if you are hungry or sleepy or want to wash,
but it is well worth it.

Another thing:  the Dictionary defines "tourist" as "one who travels for
pleasure."  This is not me.  I stay for pleasure and loathe traveling.
All of this arriving and leaving leaves little space for the kind of
strategy or social karate I enjoy.  How can one build a life out of these
cauterized place-time segments that don't fit together and preclude
both community and privacy?  My gut desire at this point is to settle
down somewhere beautiful with good friends and never even get in a car.
If anyone else is interested in this, send me a note.

The question, then, becomes 'what shall we do together?'  Two options that
I might not have been aware of before are easily done in countries other
than that we call home:  farming and teaching.  In many 'third world'
countries land is cheap (outside of cities), the technology and economy
is well developed for family-sized farms, and there is enormous demand
for teachers of english and the research arts.  But there are also many
options in America.  Some of you already live in successful utopias.
Let's talk.
