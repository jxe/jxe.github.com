Joe woke every morning with the knowledge that one thing was missing
from his life: he had love, good friends, his health, peace & quiet
whenever he wanted them, a swimming pool to use, etc... but the one
thing that he felt was most important-- high-speed internet access--
seemed forever out of reach.

Welcome to SufiNet.

Out of reach, that is, until he travelled to Ajmer, a major muslim
pilgrimage site, and met Haji Syed Mohd Hameed, Internet Sufi.
H.S.M. Hameed has been to Mecca twice.  He has been to Miami and to New
York to preach.  Americans, he says, are excellent people, but they
have lost their way.  They have lost Allah, he says, and it creates
much confusion.

Like most mystical sufis, H.S.M. Hameed is not interested in e-Commerce.
Internet startup companies, he might say, are the manifestations.
A sufi does not waste time with the manifestations, but seeks for the
roots.  H.S.M. Hameed is interested in the endless configuration and
reconfiguration of networks.  He is interested in routers and protocol
stacks.  But most of all, H.S.M. Hameed is interested in *bandwidth*.

A sufi in India with a mobile phone can make a leased line exist for
an hour, and then disappear.  He can reallocate telephone lines at
will, change copper into optical fiber, and increase bandwidth with
a quick prayer.  He is a man of absolute integrity and he will not do
it for money.  But he will do it for Allah.  He will judge you.  If you
serve God and have a strong moral code; if you are a student of the Book,
he will do it for you.

Praise the Lord.

The difference between a pilgrim and a tourist is clear:  the one lives a
life of discipline, devotion, study, and routine, while the other lives
a life of leasure, consumption, hedonism, and laziness.  The one has
a strong moral code and lives according to a religious law.  The other
is probably a hippie or a relativist and has no idea what they're doing.
The pilgrim praises the lord.  The tourist praises the view.  The pilgrim
lives in a temple or in a quiet library supported by the community.
The tourist lives in a cheap hotel run by drug dealers who serve up loud
music and terrible imitations of western food.

My first tip about travelling in India was "SIT DOWN" (Item #4).
My second is "PRAISE THE LORD."

Praise the lord and he will lift you out of the filth of tourism and
give you high-speed internet access.

The Dar al' Islam is alive and well.

	Joe
